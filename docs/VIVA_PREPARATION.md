### Q3: What problem does your project solve that existing tools do not?
**Answer:** Most commercial tools make the mistake of combining all fake reviews into one generic detector. In reality, a fake hotel review is a human lie, a fake product review might be generated by GPT-2, and a filtered restaurant review is a platform decision. My project solves this by deploying three separate, dedicated domain experts and providing mathematical word-level explanations.

### Q5: What is TF-IDF and how does it work?
**Answer:** TF-IDF stands for Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency. It converts text into numerical numbers that a machine learning model can understand.
- **Term Frequency (TF):** Measures how frequently a word appears in a review.
- **Inverse Document Frequency (IDF):** Penalizes common words (like "the", "and") that appear in almost all reviews and boosts rare, informative words.

---

### Q6: Why did you use sublinear term frequency scaling in TF-IDF?
**Answer:** Sublinear scaling replaces term frequency with $1 + \log(\text{TF})$. This prevents a word that appears 10 times from having 10 times the importance of a word that appears once, creating a more balanced and realistic feature weight.

---

### Q7: What are unigrams and bigrams, and why did you use both?
**Answer:**
- A **unigram** is a single word (e.g., "clean", "staff").
- A **bigram** is a sequence of two consecutive words (e.g., "front desk", "poor service", "not good").
Using both captures individual keywords as well as local context and negations that completely change the meaning.

---

### Q8: What is a Vectorizer?
**Answer:** A vectorizer is a tool in NLP that converts raw text strings into a mathematical matrix of numerical vectors. In our project, the `TfidfVectorizer` learns the vocabulary from the training reviews and transforms every review into a sparse numerical vector.

---

### Q9: Why did you choose Logistic Regression instead of Deep Learning or Naive Bayes?
**Answer:**
1. **Explainability:** Logistic Regression has linear weights for each word. We can directly multiply the word's TF-IDF score by its weight to explain exactly why the model made a prediction.
2. **Speed & Efficiency:** It trains in seconds and runs instantly on a laptop without requiring heavy GPUs.
3. **Strong Linear Benchmark:** For high-dimensional TF-IDF sparse text, linear classifiers are proven, robust baselines that generalize very well.

---

### Q10: What is a Confusion Matrix?
**Answer:** A confusion matrix is a table that evaluates the performance of a classification model by comparing actual labels against predicted labels:
- **True Positive (TP):** Target review correctly classified (e.g. Deceptive review correctly predicted as Deceptive).
- **True Negative (TN):** Baseline review correctly classified (e.g. Truthful review correctly predicted as Truthful).
- **False Positive (FP):** Baseline review mistakenly predicted as target (e.g. Truthful review mistakenly predicted as Deceptive; False Alarm).
- **False Negative (FN):** Target review mistakenly predicted as baseline (e.g. Deceptive review mistakenly predicted as Truthful; Missed Detection).

---

### Q11: What is Accuracy and what was your accuracy in each domain?
**Answer:** Accuracy is the percentage of total predictions that were correct:
$$\text{Accuracy} = \frac{\text{TP} + \text{TN}}{\text{Total Predictions}}$$
- **Hotel Model:** **89.69%**
- **Product Model:** **93.36%**
- **Restaurant Model:** **77.79%**

---

### Q12: What is the difference between Precision and Recall?
**Answer:**
- **Precision:** Out of all reviews the model *predicted* as fake, how many were *actually* fake? High precision means low false alarms.
- **Recall:** Out of all reviews that were *actually* fake, how many did the model *catch*? High recall means low missed fakes.

---

### Q13: What is the F1-Score?
**Answer:** The F1-Score is the harmonic mean of Precision and Recall:
$$\text{F1} = 2 \times \frac{\text{Precision} \times \text{Recall}}{\text{Precision} + \text{Recall}}$$
It gives a balanced evaluation, especially when classes are uneven.

---

### Q14: What is Balanced Accuracy?
**Answer:** Balanced accuracy is the average of recall on each individual class:
$$\text{Balanced Accuracy} = \frac{1}{2} (\text{Recall}_{\text{class0}} + \text{Recall}_{\text{class1}})$$
In balanced datasets like Ott (50/50), it equals overall accuracy. In imbalanced datasets like Yelp (77% / 23%), it reveals whether the model is only performing well on the majority class.

---

### Q15: What is Data Leakage and how did you prevent it?
**Answer:** Data leakage happens when test set information accidentally contaminates the training phase, giving falsely high test scores. We prevented it by:
1. Fitting the TF-IDF vectorizer **only** on the training partition.
2. Using `StratifiedGroupKFold` to ensure identical duplicate reviews stayed in the same split.
3. Verifying mathematically that there was **zero text-hash overlap** between train and test.

---

### Q16: Why did you keep the training and test sets strictly separate?
**Answer:** To simulate real-world deployment. A model must be tested on unseen data that it has never encountered during training or hyperparameter selection to measure its true generalization capability.

---

### Q17: Why did you train three separate models instead of one combined model?
**Answer:** Because the ground-truth meanings in the three datasets are fundamentally incompatible:
- Ott measures **human lies**.
- Amazon measures **AI-generated text (GPT-2)**.
- Yelp measures **platform recommendation decisions**.
Combining them into one model causes "construct conflation," which corrupts feature weights and invalidates predictions.

---

### Q18: What is the Ott Deceptive Opinion Spam Corpus?
**Answer:** It is the canonical benchmark dataset created by Ott et al. (ACL 2011, NAACL 2013) at Cornell University. It contains 1,600 reviews for 20 luxury Chicago hotels: 800 truthful reviews mined from TripAdvisor and 800 deceptive reviews written by Amazon Mechanical Turk workers. In our system, the hotel model evaluates this benchmark using the prediction labels **Truthful** and **Deceptive**.

---

### Q19: In the Amazon dataset, what do OR and CG mean?
**Answer:**
- **`OR`:** Original Review — human-written reviews from Amazon.
- **`CG`:** Computer-Generated Review — synthetic reviews created by fine-tuning a GPT-2 language model on Amazon reviews (Salminen et al., 2022).
Our Amazon model is implemented strictly as a TF-IDF Vectorizer + Logistic Regression pipeline that distinguishes human-written OR from GPT-2-generated CG text. It does not calculate transformer perplexity, does not use a transformer at inference, and does not independently run a repetitive n-gram detection algorithm.

---

### Q20: Why must you NOT call OR reviews "genuine" or CG reviews "fake"?
**Answer:** Because authorship is not the same as truthfulness. A real human (`OR`) might write a paid, biased review, while a customer who used an AI assistant (`CG`) might describe an honest product experience. The Amazon model distinguishes **human-written text from GPT-2-generated synthetic text**, not commercial fraud or verified honesty.

---

### Q21: In the Yelp dataset, what do N and Y mean?
**Answer:**
- **`N`:** Recommended review (prominently displayed on Yelp).
- **`Y`:** Filtered / Not Recommended review (placed in Yelp's filtered section).
Neither label confirms genuine or fake reviews.

---

### Q22: Why are Yelp's filtered reviews not necessarily fake?
**Answer:** Yelp's labels represent automated platform recommendation decisions (Recommended vs Filtered / Not Recommended). A filtered review is not necessarily fake, and a recommended review is not necessarily genuine. Platform filtering is an automated curation action, not a legal or factual verification of authenticity.

---

### Q23: Why did your Yelp model achieve 77.79% overall accuracy but low recall on filtered reviews?
**Answer:** Because the dataset exhibits natural class imbalance (76.98% Recommended vs 23.02% Filtered). The text-only classifier predicts the majority Recommended class well (98.17% recall), but has difficulty identifying Filtered reviews from text alone (9.60% recall), resulting in a Balanced Accuracy of 53.88%. Most importantly, N represents Recommended and Y represents Filtered / Not Recommended; neither label confirms genuine or fake reviews.

### Q25: Why did you use SQLite for database persistence?

### Q26: What is SQL Injection and how did you prevent it?
**Answer:** SQL Injection is a vulnerability where malicious users insert SQL commands into text inputs to manipulate or delete database data. We prevented it by using **parameterized queries** with placeholder question marks (`?`), ensuring user inputs are always treated strictly as literal data.

### Q27: How does Bulk CSV Prediction work in your application?
**Answer:** The user uploads a CSV file. The app automatically detects or lets the user choose the column containing review text. It feeds the reviews in batches through the selected domain's TF-IDF and Logistic Regression pipeline, appends prediction columns and probabilities to the DataFrame without dropping original columns, and provides a one-click CSV download.

---

### Q28: How does your mathematical feature attribution (Explainable AI) work?
**Answer:** When a review is submitted, the model vectorizes the text into active TF-IDF weights. For each active word, we multiply its TF-IDF score by the logistic regression weight for that class:
$$\text{Contribution} = \text{TF-IDF Value} \times \text{Model Coefficient}$$
If the contribution is positive, that word pushed the model towards that prediction. We sort and display the top drivers in green and red tables and highlight them in the text.

---

### Q29: What does "Model Confidence" mean in your dashboard?
**Answer:** Model confidence is simply the maximum posterior probability: $\max(P(\text{class}_0), P(\text{class}_1))$. It measures how certain the mathematical model is based on n-gram patterns in the training data. It is a statistical estimate, not ground-truth proof.

### Q30: What are the main limitations of your project?
**Answer:**
1. **Domain Vocabulary Boundaries:** The Ott hotel model predicts Truthful vs Deceptive reviews within the 20 Chicago hotels benchmark setting and cannot predict product reviews accurately because hotel terms (*bellman, suite*) do not appear in electronics reviews.
2. **Model Formulation:** The Amazon product model is implemented solely as TF-IDF Vectorizer + Logistic Regression distinguishing human-written OR from GPT-2 CG text. It does not calculate transformer perplexity, does not use a transformer at inference, and does not independently run a repetitive n-gram detection algorithm.
3. **Platform Filter Boundaries:** The Yelp model predicts Recommended (N) vs Filtered / Not Recommended (Y) curation labels from text alone; neither label confirms genuine or fake reviews.
